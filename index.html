Made for reading

Everything a good book needs, nothing it doesn’t.

One continuous scroll

No page turns. EPUB, PDF and plain-text books flow as one long page, and PDFs reflow to fit a narrow panel.

Typeset like a book

Chapters open with a drop cap and a small-caps first line, under a running head that always knows where you are.

Highlights and a notebook

Mark passages in four colours, add notes, leave a mark where you stopped, and keep a notebook for every book.

Define any word

Select a word to see what it means, from your Mac’s dictionary or Wiktionary.

Shelves and sets

Arrange your own shelves, or browse by genre. Bind a series into a set that stands as one and opens in reading order.

Fix the text

Correct a typo, mend a broken paragraph, or remove every page number and site notice at once. The file itself is never touched.

Four themes, fine type

Paper, sepia, dusk and night, with typefaces chosen for long reading. Auto-scroll when your hands are busy.

Private by design

Opening it for the first time

One extra click, once.

On a Mac

Edgeleaf isn’t signed with an Apple developer certificate yet, so macOS asks you to confirm it once:

  1. Open the download and drag Edgeleaf into Applications, then open it.
  2. macOS says it can’t check the app. Click Done.
  3. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.

From then on it opens like any other app, and lives in your menu bar.

On Android

Open the download. The first time, Android asks whether your browser may install apps: tap Settings, switch on Allow from this source, go back and tap Install. If Play Protect calls it an unrecognised app, tap More details → Install anyway.

Updates install the same way, over the top, keeping your books.

On Windows

Run the installer. If Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway.

Edgeleaf lives in the system tray (it may be under the ^ arrow). Its icon turns to an outline while it’s tucked away.

Privacy

Edgeleaf has no account and collects nothing. Your books, highlights and notes stay on your computer. When you look up a word your Mac’s dictionary doesn’t know, that one word is sent to Wiktionary.
